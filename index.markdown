# About me

I am a Mathematics undergraduate at the University of Toronto, and my intellectual curiosity is fueled by the pursuit of understanding the unknown—whether that is found in a distant backcountry vista, a philosophical text, or the architecture of a formal proof. Beyond the core of my math studies, I have found parallels between the skills required for rigorous adventure and the pursuit of certainty in logic. Just as a successful summit requires meticulous planning, self-discipline, and a constant review of potential failure modes, my work with formal logic demands an airtight approach to abstraction. This drive for comprehensive understanding is what leads me to explore the intersection of math and computer science, currently diving into proof assistants like Rocq to bridge theory with executable code.

# Writings

Here lies the collections of ideas I have written on.
+ [Spirographs and Groups](files/math/Spirographs.pdf) \
	This study investigates the implicit constraints of existing spirograph models, which often rely on the assumption of integer teeth found in gear mechanisms. By generalizing the model to consider any two circles, this paper rigorously proves the necessary and sufficient condition for a spirograph to possess group properties: the ratio of the two radii must be a rational number.
+ [Stone Representation Theorem](files/math/StoneRepresentation.pdf) \
    Systematically explores the relationship between formal logic and abstract algebra. This work defines formal systems, establishes the axioms of Boolean Algebra, and culminates in a proof of Stone’s Representation Theorem: demonstrating that every abstract Boolean Algebra is isomorphic to a concrete field of sets.
